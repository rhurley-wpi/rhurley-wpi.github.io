Recent & Current Work

ISIAQ Student Representative • HBA Autumn School Coordinator (2025)

I currently serve as the student representative to ISIAQ, working with professionals in the field of indoor air to build out student mentoring and activities. In this role I have organized a webinar focused on successful academic paper writing, facilitate the student LinkedIn page and highlight student achievements, and develop networking events for students at conferences. I am working towards expanding the scope of ISIAQ student engagement with more networking opportunities and opportunities for students to share their work with the society. I have also led student activities at ISIAQ conferences, am currently helping planning for the Indoor air 2026 summer school one-day program as well as the 2025 Healthy Building Asia Autumn School, where I coordinated a two-day international pre-conference program for students.

NSF GEOHEALTH INTERN — Aerodyne Research
Fall 2024

In the Fall of 2024 I worked as an intern with Aerodyne Research through the NSF GEOHEALTH INTERN program. This research focuses on smoke transport and the role of building material surfaces in re-emitting deposited pollutants. The project involves the use of VOCUS CI-ToF, which allows real-time mass spectrometry analysis of VOCs in indoor environments. I discuss this study in more detail on the WPI Podcast.

Podcast: Learn more with the WPI Podcast

Behavioral Response to Wildfire Smoke

Currently leading research project from data collected from undergraduate Interactive Qualifying Project. Goal to understand decision making and smoke mitigation adoption of older populations during wildfire smoke events. Project consists of semi-structured interviews and using thematic analysis to develop a novel behavior conceptual framework considering the indoor environment.

Home Ignition Zone Project — San Jose State University
Summer 2024

In summer 2024, I worked with the Home Ignition Zone Project team at San Jose State University to assess community wildfire preparedness using field surveys and drone imagery. We visited homes in three California regions and analyzed how preparedness varies with landscape and built environment characteristics.

Team: Home Ignition Zone Team
