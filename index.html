About Me

I am a third-year PhD candidate in Civil, Environmental, and Architectural Engineering at Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), where I am also affiliated with the Department of Fire Protection Engineering. Advised by Professor Shichao Liu, my research investigates the intersection of indoor air quality, wildfire smoke transport, and occupant health.


My work seeks to enhance building resilience against both indoor and outdoor pollutants. By combining laboratory-scale experiments—specifically exploring how wildfire-sourced VOCs interact with indoor materials—with behavioral psychology models, I aim to understand how the built environment and human decision-making prolong or mitigate smoke exposure. I am particularly intersted in learning more about community-engaged research and the development of evidence-based disaster readiness plans.


For many years I have been curious to conduct research and learn from different aspects of the field of engineering. Before my doctoral studies, I conducted research at Lafayette College focused on flash flooding prediction and at (WPI) investigated the cognitive impacts of CO2 exposure due to mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic. My future goal is to transition into a post-doctoral, faculty, or government position and my interests are numerous including environmental science, chemistry, environmental policy, geography and civil, mechanical, or environmental engineering.
