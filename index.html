About Me

I am a third-year PhD candidate in Civil, Environmental, and Architectural Engineering at Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), where I am also affiliated with the Department of Fire Protection Engineering. Advised by Professor Shichao Liu, my research investigates the intersection of indoor air quality, wildfire smoke transport, and occupant health.


My work seeks to enhance resilience against both indoor and outdoor pollutants. By investigating both laboratory-scale experiments, specifically exploring how wildfire-sourced VOCs interact with indoor material and behavioral psychology models, I aim to understand how the built environment, material properties and human decision-making prolong or mitigate smoke exposure. I am particularly intersted in learning more about community-engaged research and the development of evidence-based disaster readiness plans.


For many years I have been curious to conduct research and learn from different aspects of the field of engineering. Before my doctoral studies, I conducted research at Lafayette College focused on flash flooding prediction and at (WPI) investigated the cognitive impacts of CO2 exposure due to mask-wearing during the COVID-19 pandemic. My future goal is to transition into a post-doctoral, faculty, or government position in related fields including environmental science, chemistry, environmental policy, geography and civil, mechanical, or environmental engineering.

Currently leading a project to understand the decision-making and smoke mitigation adoption of older populations during wildfire events. This work utilizes semi-structured interviews and thematic analysis to develop a novel behavioral conceptual framework.
